# Voice

The house rules for my writing. The linter in `scripts/chrisnizer_lint.py` enforces
the mechanical parts. The judgment parts are here, for me or an agent to apply by
hand. These rules were distilled from my own solo writing, the AI-writing tells a
humanizer catches, and the register of my research papers. The actual writing
samples they came from stay in `VOICE.local.md`, which is untracked.

## Who is writing

I build playful, embodied human-augmentation systems: e-textiles, electrical
muscle stimulation games, wearables, WaterHCI. I write in two modes. Academic
prose for papers, where "we" and a contribution frame are correct. Direct prose
for everything else, which is what these rules are about.

## Structure

Lead with the point. Say who I am and what I want in the first two sentences. Do
not bury the lede.

Write flowing paragraphs when the content is narrative. Fold labelled fragments
into prose. Reach for a list only when the content is a list.

One idea per sentence. My first drafts run to dense compound sentences that stack
three clauses with "and". Catch those and split them.

## Tone

Professional and personable. A friendly opener, a direct ask, a plain sign-off.
Not stiff, not overly casual.

No boosterism. Nothing is groundbreaking, seamless, or best-in-class, and no
effort is a big lift.

Confident and matter-of-fact. State what I want plainly. Do not hedge.

## Mechanics

No em dashes. Use a comma, a period, or a colon.

Active voice. "The model reads the gaze path" beats "the gaze path is read by the
model." Name the actor.

Minimal filler. I cut it from AI drafts, so I avoid it in my own.

First person singular for solo work. "I" and "my", not "we" and "our", unless it
is a co-authored paper.

## Calibration

These rules are inferred from a handful of samples. The rhythm and sentence-length
rules in particular sharpen with a longer unedited sample. Those samples live in
`samples/` and `VOICE.local.md`, both untracked; revise these rules against them.
